# Transformation in consultancy

- Transformation means helping an organisation make a large, lasting change to how it works, not a
  one-off fix or single project.
- Big sector because the work is large, runs for years and is billed at scale.
- One of the main revenue sources for big firms (McKinsey, BCG, Accenture, Deloitte etc.).

## Common types

- **Digital (largest):** moving processes, products and customer channels onto modern technology
  (e.g. cloud migration, data platforms, automation). AI transformation is its newest form.
- **Operating model:** changing structure and running of the organisation: teams, roles, decision
  rights, ways of working (e.g. agile at scale).
- **Business/strategic:** changing what the company does or how it makes money, often after a market
  shift or new leadership.
- **Cost/performance:** large efficiency programmes, often with restructuring.
- **Cultural:** changing behaviours and values, usually alongside one of the above.

## Why it's so large

- **Scale and length:** multi-year programmes with many consultants; one client can be worth tens of
  millions.
- **Spans many skills:** strategy, technology, change management and programme management sold under
  one banner.
- **Need never ends:** each technology wave (internet, mobile, cloud, now AI) creates a new round.
- **Most programmes fall short:** commonly cited figure is ~70% fail to meet their goals. This is
  partly why clients buy outside help and why the work recurs.

## The criticism

- The term is vague enough to cover almost anything; it can mean expensive change programmes with
  little lasting effect.
- Its value in practice depends on whether the client's own people work differently after the
  consultants leave.
